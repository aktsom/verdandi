## Crash in PipeWire's libjack with Verdandi:midi_out (KNOWN-ISSUES 35)
- **Cause:** pipewire-jack 1.0.5 converts a cycle's output events into pods in a 32 768-byte scratch and copies the *counted* size, also when it is larger: our callback filled the JACK port buffer (about 4000 small events), so a burst crashed it. Reproduced headless under `pw-jack` with the same offsets and a 57 712-byte memcpy.
- **Fixed:** at most 1000 events and 24 000 bytes of pods per cycle, the rest waits in the queue; drops are counted and said at most every 10 s from outside the callback. `midi_in` read for the same fault: nothing found.
- **Tests first:** `jack_midi_out_stress.cc` under PipeWire's JACK layer and jackd in `run_all.sh`, budget checks in `midi_frame_test.c`.

## The virtual MIDI device as a native ALSA sequencer port ("Verdandi" / "Verdandi out")
- **Built (Linux desktop only):** `matron/src/platform/linux/aseq_out.{h,cc}` and `aseq_pump.h`. An ALSA sequencer client "Verdandi" with one readable, subscribable port "Verdandi out" (type MIDI generic): a DAW that reads ALSA MIDI only (Reaper on Linux) lists it as a MIDI input device, no JACK bridge. It is fed by the same send path as `Verdandi:midi_out` (`dev_midi_send` of the "virtual" device calls both; either may be missing): notes, CC, program change, pitch bend, key and channel pressure, song position and select, quarter frame, tune request, clock, start, continue, stop, active sensing, reset and sysex (long ones in pieces of at most 512 bytes, as the JACK output). The sender never waits: bytes are cut into messages (`midi_frame.h`) into a bounded lock-free queue (2048) and a worker thread delivers them in order with `snd_seq_event_output_direct` (direct delivery: the order is kept, the time is the worker's, not frame-accurate like the JACK port); a full destination (-EAGAIN) is waited for 2 ms up to 4 tries, then the event is dropped and counted; drops are said `aseq_out: N message(s) dropped ...` at most every 10 s from outside the delivery. Nothing is connected automatically, nothing is sent unless a script sends.
- **Setting:** `alsa_midi=0` in `audio.conf` or `NORNS_ALSA_MIDI=0` turns it off; on by default (`linux/norns.sh` exports `NORNS_ALSA_MIDI`). Without a kernel sequencer norns says so once and goes on. The launcher, the plug-in check and the other MIDI paths are untouched.
- **Own client, no loop:** it is no norns MIDI device (the device list comes from udev's rawmidi nodes; the "virtual" device is the JACK one), and a bridge's JACK port for it ("Midi-Bridge:Verdandi:Verdandi out", "a2j:Verdandi ...") is skipped by `midi_in_from` / `midi_out_to` because norns' own ALSA clients are found by their process id (`ownAlsaNames`; new cases in `tests/crone/test_midi_route.cpp`). **No input port was built** ("Verdandi in" was optional): the clock path of `midi_in` is timed by the JACK reader, a second writer into the same pipe would have needed more than could be tested here, and a DAW's clock and notes keep using `Verdandi:midi_in`.
- **Tests (written first):** `aseq_pump_test.cpp` (14 cases with a fake sequencer: every message type and its event, channel, pitch bend range, order of 1000, nothing sent = nothing touched, no subscribers is not an error, a sender never blocks and the queue is bounded, -EAGAIN retries, errors counted without a crash, rate-limited log, long sysex, the worker); `aseq_out_test.cpp` (the real client with libasound: off by `NORNS_ALSA_MIDI=0`, `-1` without a sequencer and with it unstarted); the route cases. The JACK output, input and auto-connect tests are unchanged and run as before.

The end-to-end receiver (`aseq_dump_test.c` with `probe_aseq_out.lua`, an aseqdump-style client that must get the notes, CC, clock, start/stop and sysex in order and nothing when nothing is sent) is written and in `run_all.sh`, but it skips itself without `/dev/snd/seq`; it has never run. Also unchecked: Reaper listing "Verdandi out", the event types a real DAW accepts (clock and start/stop events carry no queue), delivery under load on a real sequencer.
